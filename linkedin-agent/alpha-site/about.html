Visibility on a professional network is not won by posting more often.

Brandon, Founder, majiklTrust Market Intelligence, LLC. Illinois, USA.

It is won by showing up consistently with something worth reading, and most businesses cannot sustain that because the research is the expensive part. majikl inverts that cost.

Already researched before you read it

The platform watches the sources your market actually reads, feed by curated feed, and turns what it finds into drafts that arrive already researched, already attributed to a named primary source, and already scored for quality. Your team's job narrows to the one decision only a human should make: what the company says.

From that single pipeline an approved post can publish to the company page, to a leader's personal profile, or to both, and land in a scheduled slot chosen because your own audience data says that hour performs. The result is a steady, defensible presence in the feeds where your buyers form their opinions, produced without a writing staff and without a content calendar that quietly goes empty during a busy quarter.

Reach compounds through people your audience already trusts

The larger gain in reach comes from the people who already work there. A company page reaches the followers it has. An employee's profile reaches the network that person spent a career building, and those are the accounts that turn attention into conversation. majikl takes one published company post and fans it into individual variants, one for each colleague who has consented and connected their own account, each shaped by that person's own voice notes and each published under their own identity only after they have read it and approved it.

Nothing goes out in someone's name that they did not see. Every claim traces back to a source, every figure in a data post is verified before publication is allowed, and every outcome returns as measured engagement, follower demographics, and hour-by-hour performance that resets what the next cycle publishes and when. Reach compounds here because the program is honest: your audience grows through voices it already trusts, and your content sharpens each cycle because evidence is teaching it rather than guesswork.

Built by someone who does not publish what he cannot verify

Our founder is the engineer who set and enforces the standard on every line of the platform, from the database to the page you are reading now.

Brandon has spent his career in the space where software meets trust: building systems where being wrong is expensive and being unverifiable is worse. Somewhere along the way a professional habit hardened into a personal rule: a claim is worth exactly as much as the trail behind it. Confident statements get checked against the source. Every time. Even his own, and especially the ones that sound most certain.

Market Intelligence is that rule, written in code. He watched AI tools learn to write faster than they learned to be right, and watched professionals pay for it with the only currency that matters on a professional network: credibility. So he built the tool he was willing to put his own name behind. It reads continuously from sources you choose, corroborates before it drafts, cites what it read, and then stops. It does not publish. You do. The platform's job ends where your judgment begins, and that boundary is load bearing.

He made the whole thing himself: the research pipeline, the drafting engine, the encryption, the review queue, the infrastructure it all runs on. Not because help is unwelcome, but because accountability should have a name on it. When something is wrong, you are talking to the person who can fix it, usually the same day.

The principles the platform is held to

  • The human has the first, last, and final word. No post reaches your page without your explicit approval. Brand safety is a step you perform, not a setting you hope is on.
  • Verify first, always. Nothing is drafted from thin air. Research is grounded in the sources you trust and corroborated across independent reporting before a word is written.
  • Fail closed. When the platform is uncertain, it stops and says so. Silence and pretending are failure modes here, treated exactly as seriously as crashes.
  • Earn trust, do not assert it. The name is a promise with a working definition: trust that can be traced, checked, and re earned on every single post.
